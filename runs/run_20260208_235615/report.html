Confounders

Potential confounding variables in causal relationships.

Total: 15

How This Method Works

The Confounder Detector searches for third variables connected to both ends of a suspected causal relation. For each candidate causal pair A → C, it identifies entities B that share edges with both A and C, scoring B by how strongly it is associated with each endpoint and how likely it is to be a genuine shared cause rather than a coincidental neighbor.

Why it matters: Confounders can make spurious correlations appear causal. Identifying them early guides researchers to control for these variables in statistical or experimental analyses, preventing false causal conclusions and improving the credibility of derived hypotheses.

Why it matters: Logical entailments can significantly densify the knowledge graph with reliable implicit knowledge — facts that are not explicitly stated but necessarily follow from what is.
