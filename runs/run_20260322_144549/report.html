Confounders

Potential confounding variables in causal relationships.

Total: 15

How This Method Works

The Confounder Detector searches for third variables connected to both ends of a suspected causal relation. For each candidate causal pair A → C, it identifies entities B that share edges with both A and C, scoring B by how strongly it is associated with each endpoint and how likely it is to be a genuine shared cause rather than a coincidental neighbor.

Why it matters: Confounders can make spurious correlations appear causal. Identifying them early guides researchers to control for these variables in statistical or experimental analyses, preventing false causal conclusions and improving the credibility of derived hypotheses.

Logical Entailments

Total: 20

How This Method Works

The Logical Entailment detector applies a set of first-order inference rules to derive implicit facts from the graph. For example: if A is-a B and B has-property P, then A has-property P. Rules are applied iteratively until a fixed point, and each derived fact is scored by the confidence of the rule chain that produced it.

Why it matters: Logical entailments can significantly densify the knowledge graph with reliable implicit knowledge — facts that are not explicitly stated but necessarily follow from what is. They reduce redundant explicit assertions needed and reveal implied relations that improve downstream reasoning quality without requiring additional data collection.
